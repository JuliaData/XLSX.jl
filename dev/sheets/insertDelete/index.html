Inserting and deleting rows and columns

XLSX.insertRows, XLSX.deleteRows, XLSX.insertColumns and XLSX.deleteColumns work as Excel's Insert and Delete commands do: the cells below (or to the right) move, and everything in the workbook that refers to them follows. Open the file with mode="rw" (or start from XLSX.newxlsx).

XLSX.openxlsx("budget.xlsx", mode="rw") do xf
    sheet = xf["Budget"]
    XLSX.insertRows(sheet, 5)              # a new, empty row 5
    XLSX.insertRows(sheet, 10; count=3)    # three new rows, 10 to 12
    XLSX.deleteRows(sheet, "20:22")
    XLSX.insertColumns(sheet, "C")
    XLSX.deleteColumns(sheet, [2, 6])      # columns B and F
end

Rows and columns to delete can be given as numbers, ranges, vectors, or strings naming rows ("3:5,9"), columns ("B:C"), cells or a defined name.

What follows the moved cells

  • Formulas in any sheet of the workbook, whether their references are relative or absolute ($A$5 moves too, as in Excel).
  • Row heights, hidden and grouped rows, and column widths.
  • Merged cells, conditional formats, data validations, hyperlinks, the AutoFilter, frozen panes and page breaks.
  • Defined names, including the print area and print titles.
  • The ranges plotted by charts.
  • Images, charts and comments on the sheet (see below).

A range that spans the place where rows are inserted grows to include them: =SUM(A1:A10) becomes =SUM(A1:A11) when a row is inserted at row 5. Inserting just after a range leaves it as it is, as in Excel, with one exception: see formatting below.

How new rows and columns are formatted

As in Excel, whose default is Format Same As Above, a new row is formatted like the row above it, and a new column like the column to its left:

  • each cell takes the style of the cell next to it: its number format, font, fill, alignment and protection. Borders are not copied: a cell in a new row has the border of its column's style, if the column has one, and a cell in a new column the border of its row's style;
  • a row takes the height, row style and outline level of the row above, and a column the width, style and outline level of the column to its left. Neither is hidden, even when the row or column it copies is;
  • a row or column inserted inside a hidden group is shown, and since the group is no longer all hidden, its summary row or column is no longer marked collapsed;
  • a conditional format or data validation that ends at the row above (the column to the left) grows to cover the new rows (columns), since they now have that formatting too. Merged cells, tables, the AutoFilter, hyperlinks and defined names don't.

The new cells hold no values or formulas. Rows inserted at row 1, and columns at column A, have nothing to copy and are left unformatted.

Deleting

A range that loses some of its rows shrinks. A reference to a cell that is deleted becomes #REF!:

julia> XLSX.getFormula(sheet, "F2")
"=A5*2"

julia> XLSX.deleteRows(sheet, 5);

julia> XLSX.getFormula(sheet, "F2")
"=#REF!*2"

A merged range, conditional format, data validation, hyperlink or AutoFilter whose cells are all deleted goes too.

Tables on the sheet

An Excel Table grows to take in rows or columns inserted inside it, and shrinks as they are deleted from it. Rows or columns inserted at its first row or column move it instead, and ones inserted just after it leave it as it is, as in Excel.

Columns inserted inside a table become table columns, named Column1, Column2, ... in its header row; rename them by writing new headers. Deleting a table column deletes its references: =SUM(Sales[Amount]) becomes =SUM(#REF!) in every formula, defined name, conditional format, data validation and chart. A table all of whose rows or columns are deleted is deleted, and references to it become #REF! too.

Some deletions are refused, because a table can't be left without them:

  • its header row, without the rest of the table;
  • its totals row: remove it with XLSX.removetotals! first;
  • all its data rows: a table keeps at least one.

Images, charts and comments

Images, charts and shapes move with the cells they sit over, according to their Properties in Excel's Format pane:

  • Move and size with cells (the default for a chart): rows or columns inserted under the object stretch it, and ones deleted under it shrink it. If all of them are deleted, the object is deleted, as in Excel, with its picture or chart part unless something else uses it.
  • Move but don't size with cells: the object moves, keeping its size, even when all its rows or columns are deleted.
  • Don't move or size with cells: the object stays where it is.

A comment moves with its cell, and its box with it. A comment on a deleted cell is deleted. A threaded comment (Excel 365) also moves with its cell, but deleting the cell it is on is refused, so that a conversation isn't lost by accident: delete it first with XLSX.deleteThreadedComment.

Limits

  • Inserting rows or columns that would push data past the last row or column Excel allows is an error, as in Excel.
  • References written as text, such as INDIRECT("A5"), and references in other workbooks don't change, as in Excel. Nor do 3-D references (Sheet1:Sheet3!A5).
  • A shared formula (one Excel stores once for a block of cells) stays shared when the edit moves all its cells alike. One the edit would change unevenly, say because its cells refer across the inserted rows, or rows inside its block are deleted, is stored as a separate formula in each of its cells afterwards. The formulas are the same; the file may be a little larger.
